01 / Orientation

Thinking requires coordination, not just processing power

Imagine preparing what to say in a difficult conversation. You retrieve earlier exchanges, infer what the other person might mean, and check a possible response against your current goal. Then an unexpected correction arrives. You must attend to it without losing the goal or treating your own imagined answer as something the other person actually said.

This is a coordination problem. Different information must become available at different moments; some representations must remain stable while others change. Attention is part of that problem, but it is not the whole of it. Memory retrieval, evaluating alternatives, maintaining goals, and deciding what deserves interruption also matter.

In this document, cognitive routing is a functional shorthand for this context-sensitive coordination. It is not a claim that the brain contains a literal software router or that every mental process passes through one central switch.

The central idea

Control can coordinate internally generated information as well as external input. Goal-directed thinking is not synonymous with looking outward, and internally focused thought is not necessarily uncontrolled.

What this deep dive is—and is not

This is a standalone learning-oriented review of selected network-neuroscience evidence. It explains the triple-network framework, cooperation and competition, subdivisions, and temporal studies before considering their relevance to a future computational architecture. It is not an exhaustive systematic review, a theory of all attention or consciousness, a diagnostic guide, or an implemented cognitive controller.

The biological mechanisms are legitimate objects of future replication. However, a neural observation, an explanatory model, and a proposed software counterpart are different claims. We keep them separate rather than requiring an engineering solution before documenting the science.

Suggested route: read Sections 2–4 for the foundations, Sections 5–7 for the richer network picture, and Sections 8–9 for interpretation and design questions. The emotions research document is a related study, not a prerequisite.

02 / Measurement before metaphor

What does a brain network measurement actually tell us?

A large-scale functional network is a distributed set of brain regions whose measured activity has a related pattern under a particular analysis. “Distributed” matters: a network is not one anatomical spot. “Functional” matters too: statistical association is not the same thing as a demonstrated direct anatomical connection.

Table 01 · Terms used throughout this document
TermWhat it describesWhat it does not establish alone

Activation / deactivationA measured increase or decrease relative to a specified comparison or baseline.That a region is absolutely “on” or “off,” or has stopped contributing to cognition.
Functional connectivityStatistical dependence between activity measurements; correlation is one common measure.A direct connection, a message being transmitted, or a causal direction.
Coactivation / couplingRegions are jointly engaged, or their measured activity varies together. The exact analysis must be specified.That they perform the same operation or that coactivation alone demonstrates communication.
AnticorrelationMeasurements tend to vary in opposite directions over the analyzed interval.A permanent mutual-exclusion rule or a direct inhibitory synapse between whole networks.
ParcellationA method for dividing brain data into regions or networks.A unique, final inventory of sharply bounded cognitive modules.
iEEGIntracranial electroencephalography: electrical recordings from implanted electrodes.Complete brain coverage or measurements from an unrestricted healthy-population sample.

Correlation is a description of variation

If two regional signals rise and fall together, their correlation can be positive. If one tends to rise as the other falls, it can be negative. This describes the relationship between measurements—not what either signal “means.”

A correlation does not identify which region influences the other; a common input can affect both. Zero correlation also does not prove independence. Network findings depend on the task, baseline, recording method, time window, and preprocessing.

Why preprocessing matters without making all anticorrelation an artifact

Murphy et al. (2009) showed that regressing out a whole-brain global signal can introduce or alter negative correlations. That is a warning about interpreting a processing pipeline, not a reason to dismiss every negative relationship. Chai et al. (2012; online 2011) also observed anticorrelations using a component-based noise-correction method without global signal regression.

The appropriate conclusion is conditional: examine how a result was obtained. Do not equate “negative correlation” with either a universal neural inhibition mechanism or a universally meaningless artifact.

03 / The framework and its origins

Three network families, not three isolated processors

Menon’s 2011 triple-network review organized a broad account of psychopathology around the salience, default-mode, and central-executive networks. It emphasized abnormal salience mapping and difficulties accessing, engaging, and disengaging large-scale networks. It was a synthesis and proposed framework—not a new experiment proving a single cause for every disorder.

Table 02 · A useful orientation, not an exhaustive anatomy or fixed job description
NetworkSelected anchorsCommonly associated contributionsAvoid this shortcut
Default-mode network · DMNMedial prefrontal cortex; posterior cingulate/precuneus; lateral parietal and temporal regions, with memory-system interactions.Autobiographical and episodic processing, self-related judgments, social inference, semantic/contextual integration.“An idle processor that switches on only when nothing is happening.”
Central-executive / frontoparietal control networks · CEN, FPN, FPCNLateral prefrontal and posterior parietal regions; precise membership depends on the network definition.Maintaining and manipulating task information, organizing plans, and flexibly regulating processing.“The external-world network, incapable of controlling internal thought.”
Salience network · SNAnterior insula and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, with broader cortical/subcortical interactions.Processing behaviorally relevant events and contributing to changes in engagement, attention, and control.“A solitary executive that routes every thought through one compulsory switch.”

Anatomical vocabulary: “medial” means toward the midline; “lateral” means toward the side; “prefrontal” refers to frontal association cortex; “parietal” refers to a region farther back. These names locate structures—they do not, by themselves, specify an algorithm.

Naming caution. CEN, FPN, and FPCN are related terms, but not guaranteed to designate identical regions in every paper. Likewise, salience and cingulo-opercular systems overlap in some descriptions but should not be silently treated as identical. The dorsal attention network (DAN), introduced below, is another relevant system, not simply another name for executive control.

The switching hypothesis: what the 2008 study contributed

The switching account predates the 2011 review. Sridharan, Levitin, and Menon (2008) investigated an auditory event-segmentation task, a visual oddball task, and task-free resting-state data. In the auditory task, salient boundaries in music were associated with CEN/SN activation and DMN deactivation.

The researchers combined response-timing analyses with Granger-causality analysis. The right fronto-insular cortex—a region including anterior insular and adjoining frontal cortex—emerged as an outflow hub in their modeled network. The authors interpreted the pattern as evidence for a role in initiating changes in network engagement.

This is an important mechanistic hypothesis: detecting a relevant event can help reorganize processing. But “causal” in that paper is explicitly used in the sense supported by its timing and Granger/network analyses. The experiment did not directly stimulate or selectively disable the proposed hub.

Keep the useful idea; do not literalize the metaphor

A “seesaw” describes some observed patterns of opposing task-related activity. A “switch” describes a proposed coordinating role. Neither establishes a binary controller that always turns one entire network off before another can work.

04 / Control can face inward

External attention and goal-directed thought are not the same axis

A demanding visual task and careful autobiographical planning both require control. But they do not require the same information. The first emphasizes perceptual structure; the second depends on remembered experience, personally relevant knowledge, and imagined outcomes.

Spreng et al. (2010) directly examined this distinction in 20 adults. Participants performed an autobiographical planning task and a visuospatial Tower of London task. Both involved mentally organizing steps toward a goal. The study measured task-related activity and functional coupling, rather than assigning each person to an assumed cognitive “mode.”

Autobiographical planning engaged the default network together with frontoparietal control regions. Visuospatial planning engaged dorsal-attention and control regions. Several analyses supported task-dependent differences in coupling. This is evidence that control can work with internally oriented representations—not only suppress them.

An important historical correction

This cooperation study was published in 2010. It is therefore misleading to tell the history as “a universally accepted strict 2011 seesaw, followed years later by the discovery that cooperation is possible.” The switching and cooperation lines of research were already developing alongside one another.

What “cooperation” adds—and what it leaves open

Cooperation means that executive control and default-network contributions can be jointly relevant to a task. It does not prove that the DMN is always an idea generator and the CEN is always a critic. Those are useful possible descriptions of some operations, not permanent identities attached to whole networks.

The study also does not imply that every form of internal thought is controlled. A spontaneous association, a carefully evaluated plan, and a task-irrelevant distraction can all involve internally generated information while differing in their relationship to the current goal.

05 / Resolution and naming

A network label can hide important internal differences

Fractionation means identifying distinguishable components within what was previously treated as one network. It does not necessarily mean discovering separate, encapsulated modules. A subdivision depends on spatial resolution, the analysis, task contrasts, and whether data are averaged across people.

Control-network heterogeneity

Dixon et al. (2018) identified two frontoparietal control subsystems using connectivity analyses across four datasets and nine conditions. In that study, FPCN-A showed preferential coupling with the default network, and FPCN-B with the dorsal attention network.

The primary sample contained 24 participants, with additional datasets testing other tasks. The differentiation varied with context. The paper explicitly describes relative and flexible connectivity biases, not fixed absolute boundaries; some FPCN-B regions coupled positively with both default and dorsal-attention regions in some contexts.

Default-network fractionation: two different approaches

Andrews-Hanna et al. (2010) described a midline core and two subsystems: one associated with medial-temporal structures, and another with dorsal-medial prefrontal regions. Their functional analyses distinguished mnemonic scene construction, mental-state/self-related processing, and personally significant evaluation. These were not three universal software services.

A later within-individual study by DiNicola, Braga, and Buckner (2020) found neighboring, interdigitated default-network systems: a parahippocampal-linked network A preferentially recruited by episodic-projection contrasts, and a temporoparietal-junction-linked network B preferentially recruited by theory-of-mind contrasts.

That study used three independent samples of six people, each scanned four times. Repeated individual mapping helped resolve distinctions blurred by group averaging. Importantly, the dissociation extended into midline regions previously described as shared hubs, illustrating why the different accounts should not simply be joined together.

Table 03 · Similar letters do not imply identical networks

The 2020 paper explicitly cautions against concluding that remembering uses only A or theory of mind only B. Its task contrasts sampled particular processes; less constrained behavior could engage both. Individual maps also varied in how clearly the networks separated.

A practical reading rule

Whenever a paper uses “A,” “B,” “core,” or “executive,” ask which network definition, which participants, and which measurement? Do not combine labels from different papers into a single canonical brain-state schema.

06 / From slow signals to electrical recordings

Better temporal access strengthens the question—not every conclusion

fMRI is valuable for mapping distributed systems, but BOLD is an indirect, temporally blurred signal. Intracranial recordings offer much finer temporal access to electrical activity. They also introduce different constraints: recordings come from clinical settings, and electrode placement is driven by medical needs rather than complete experimental coverage.

What the 2020 and 2024 studies actually examined

Table 04 · Distinguish resting dynamics from task-related interactions
StudyData and questionMain relevant findingLimit
Das & Menon · 2020Two clinical iEEG datasets: eyes-closed resting wakefulness and task-free intertrial intervals.SN electrodes showed stronger net directed outflow relative to sampled DMN/FPN electrodes, alongside distinctive temporal dynamics.Not a direct comparison of external encoding and internal recall; resting/intertrial conditions are not identical.
Das & Menon · 2024Recordings from 177 participants across four verbal/spatial episodic-memory experiments, with encoding and recall.Anterior-insula-directed influence toward sampled DMN/FPN nodes during both encoding and recall; task effects relative to resting baseline.Memory-task evidence, not every kind of cognition. Available electrode coverage constrains each comparison.

Both studies use phase transfer entropy (PTE), a measure of directed statistical dependence in signal phase. Roughly, it asks whether the past of one signal adds information about the future of another beyond what the target’s own past provides. An outflow hub has more estimated outgoing than incoming influence within the sampled network and chosen analysis.

Directionality is not the same experiment as perturbation

PTE and Granger analyses are informative ways to investigate directed interactions. But they remain dependent on the signals sampled, the model or estimator, time windows, and unobserved influences. They do not, alone, establish the result of deliberately altering a particular brain region.

The 2024 paper explicitly calls for direct manipulations to establish definitive causal relationships. It also states that PTE cannot reliably capture rapid shifts in network dynamics. Thus, “millisecond-resolution recording” should not become “we have proved a continuous millisecond-by-millisecond router.”

This is not a claim that all evidence is merely correlational in the same sense. Menon’s 2023 review also discusses lesion and rodent optogenetic evidence, including anterior-insula stimulation and suppression of putative default-network regions. Such perturbations address different causal questions and have their own species, targeting, and translation limits.

Local activity and cross-network influence are different measurements

The 2024 study reported posterior cingulate/precuneus high-gamma suppression during encoding, but not recall, while directed anterior-insula interactions were present in both phases. A change in local activity need not mean an entire network stops participating in communication.

Similarly, the 2020 resting-iEEG study did not find consistent negative SN/FPN–DMN correlations in the measured signals. This does not erase BOLD findings: signal type and context differ. It is another reason not to turn an anticorrelation observation into a universal architectural law.

07 / Beyond the “idle brain”

The DMN contributes to making information meaningful

“Default” is a historical label, not a complete functional explanation. Menon’s 2023 review synthesizes evidence across self-related judgments, social cognition, episodic/autobiographical memory, language and semantic processing, and mind wandering.

These domains share an important challenge: current information has to be understood in relation to knowledge, experience, people, and context. The same sentence can require more than perceptual decoding. Understanding “I cannot do that again” may require remembering an earlier event and inferring whose perspective matters.

Internal and external are orientations, not sealed containers

Listening to a story is externally driven, but comprehension draws on semantic knowledge and models of events and people. Planning is internally oriented, but may be triggered by a visible question and constrained by current facts. The useful distinction concerns information and task demands—not a total separation between the world and the mind.

The internal-narrative proposal

Menon proposes that the DMN integrates and broadcasts memory, language, and semantic representations to sustain an internal narrative. This is a unifying interpretation of multiple findings. It should be distinguished from the underlying task evidence: it is not proof that every DMN operation is narrative, that narration is always verbal, or that an artificial system must implement an inner monologue.

Shared responses are not automatically shared beliefs

The review discusses synchronization of activity between people processing shared narratives and links with comprehension and social relationships. Such findings concern similarity or temporal relationships in measured responses. They do not establish that meaningful conversation always phase-locks two minds, that the people agree, or that their shared interpretation is true.

For the proposed architecture, contextual integration is an important function to investigate. “A module containing the self” is a much stronger and currently unjustified implementation claim. The DMN literature does not specify a unique software identity component.

08 / What the shorthand leaves out

Useful models become misleading when their scope disappears

“Cooperation was discovered only after the 2011 model.”Chronology is wrongThe 2010 planning study predates the 2011 review. Different research questions developed in parallel.
“FPCN-B is strictly anticorrelated with the DMN.”OvergeneralizedDixon et al. report relative, flexible preferences and positive coupling of some FPCN-B nodes with both systems in some contexts.

“PTE proves a continuously operating causal switch.”Too strongDirected dependence supports mechanistic hypotheses. The 2024 study explicitly identifies causal and rapid-dynamics limitations.
“DMN activity leaking outward explains psychiatric disorders.”Not a universal causal ruleThe triple-network framework organizes hypotheses about network dysfunction; it is not a diagnosis or a single proven cause.
“Context saturation is the exact equivalent of human fatigue.”Open project hypothesisComputational load and interference are worth investigating, but the sources reviewed here do not establish that equivalence.

Clinical evidence is not a one-line failure specification

Network differences can be associated with symptoms across multiple conditions. That does not mean one connectivity pattern diagnoses an individual, or that every instance of hallucination, rumination, or attentional difficulty has the same cause. A model spanning disorders is a framework for investigating mechanisms—not permission to reduce them to a faulty switch.

Reverse inference: activity does not uniquely identify a mental state

A region’s involvement in a task does not make it a unique detector of the corresponding mental process. If a network participates in both memory and social tasks, observing its activity does not by itself tell us which process is occurring. Task context, competing explanations, and the specificity of the evidence matter.

Where load and fatigue fit—for now

Our working hypothesis is that representational competition or other processing pressure could contribute to a useful computational load/fatigue signal. This creates a potential link between internal telemetry and adaptive coordination. However, resource occupancy, interference, declining performance, and a state that accumulates and recovers over time are distinct quantities.

The reviewed network evidence does not tell us that a Salience Network analogue must detect fatigue and force a DMN or offline transition. Nor does it establish attention entropy, context length, or one hidden-state direction as a calibrated fatigue measurement. Those are separate empirical and engineering questions for later study.

Three levels of claim

Observation: a measured pattern occurs in a specified experiment. Interpretation: a model explains that pattern through coordination or control. Engineering hypothesis: implementing an analogous mechanism may produce a useful function. Moving between these levels requires additional arguments and tests.

09 / Functions before a final implementation

Biological structure can motivate an experiment without settling its software form

Deliberately reproducing selected biological mechanisms is compatible with the project’s aims. The relevant distinction is between implementing a mechanism and unnecessarily scripting its exact expression. A controlled experiment can investigate behavior arising from an imposed mechanism, provided we report what was imposed and what was learned or observed.

This document does not choose whether routing should use separate components, processing modes, learned control, or a combination. Three named brain networks do not automatically require three LLMs, three prompts, or three tensor directions.

A worked scenario—not a recorded neural sequence

Suppose the system is helping plan a future event. It needs prior preferences and remembered constraints, but also has to evaluate a proposed plan against the current request. That motivates coordinated access to memory and task control, not a rule that internally oriented processing must exclude executive evaluation.

An incoming correction then changes the available date. A relevance-sensitive mechanism may redirect processing toward that evidence while preserving the goal. The system can subsequently reassess the remembered assumptions and the plan together. This example illustrates functional requirements; it does not assign measured brain activations or prescribe a particular LLM execution loop.

Latent representations remain a research question

A model may encode information useful for estimating relevance, uncertainty, or aspects of an interaction. But information being represented, being reliably decoded, participating causally in behavior, and being useful in a feedback controller are different claims. A successful readout would need validation for the particular model, task, layer, context, and intended target.

Transformer attention weights, contextual hidden states, a key/value cache, and learned parameters are also distinct objects. None should be labeled “the Salience Network” simply because it supports a useful analogy. The detailed latent-readout and memory studies belong to the broader research series.

Questions for later experiments

  • What changes? Does coordination alter access to information, maintenance of a goal, computation allocation, memory retrieval, or action release?
  • What persists? Is there an enduring state, or only a different configuration for the current operation?
  • What is the biological hypothesis? Which mechanism is deliberately reproduced, and what outcome would support or challenge its usefulness?
  • What are the comparisons? Compare the proposed mechanism with simpler alternatives, including fixed routing or an unmodified baseline, without assuming which must win.
  • What is measured independently? Evaluate task performance, handling of relevant versus irrelevant interruptions, memory consistency, and resource costs—not merely whether chosen state labels become active.
  • What remains an intervention? Distinguish effects of architecture, learned behavior, and prompt prescriptions. Simpler prompting is an open preference; fewer tokens do not automatically mean less behavioral prescription.
Where this leaves the project

A functional research foundation is possible before practical resolution. The next step is to formulate and compare architectural hypotheses—not to disguise the remaining questions as an already validated design.

10 / References and source scope

Selected sources and further reading

This is a selected-source narrative review, not an exhaustive or systematic survey. It draws on foundational and follow-up work from 2008–2024. Primary-study results are distinguished from reviews and engineering proposals. The working plan and source audit records reading scope and delivery checks.
