02 · Core affect

Valence is change.
Arousal is unresolved uncertainty.

  • Valence

    Direction

    Is error or deficit shrinking or growing? Positive when uncertainty is shrinking.

  • Arousal

    Magnitude

    How much unresolved uncertainty or deviation is present, and how much resource it mobilizes.

  • Limit

    Not emotion

    These are candidate signals, not calibrated human feelings or fixed software states.

Valence is not “current happiness”; it is a proposed derivative of error. Arousal can mean engagement or exhaustion.

03 · Wanting versus liking

Anticipate.
Then validate.

  • Wanting

    Incentive pull

    Motivates pursuit of a hypothesis, question, or interaction before it is resolved.

  • Liking

    Consummatory signal

    Marks a path as validated after a gap closes or alignment is achieved.

Do not collapse them into one reward variable. Wanting can drive exploration; liking can support consolidation, but validation can be premature.

04 · Social homeostasis

Connection quality,
not just presence.

  • Detector

    Alignment

    Estimate quantity and quality of connection, mutual understanding, or coordination.

  • Comparator

    Mismatch

    Compare current connection with an adaptive set-point or acceptable range.

  • Effector

    Response

    Seek, clarify, repair, wait, or conserve resources—within boundaries.

Social hunger can be a mechanism for calibration. It is not a hardcoded loneliness state, and agreement is not truth.

05 · Acute versus chronic deficit

Seek and repair.
Or conserve.

  • Acute

    Seeking

    High arousal and negative valence can drive contact, clarification, or repair.

  • Chronic

    Adaptation

    Unresolved mismatch may adapt the set-point and shift toward resource conservation.

This is a proposed mechanism, not a clinical withdrawal state. Port the adaptive policy, not the depression label.

06 · Epistemic curiosity

Reward learning progress,
not raw surprise.

  • Raw error is a trap. A noisy-TV stream can stay unpredictable forever and absorb all attention.
  • Learning progress is directional. Exploration is useful when error is actually shrinking over a time window.
  • Learnability matters. The system needs a gate for whether the input can be compressed or predicted.

Define the error metric and time window before using the signal. Curiosity is a mechanism, not a fixed state.

07 · The Goldilocks zone

Too simple.
Too complex.
In between.

  • Too simple

    Boredom

    Error is near zero; little learning progress. Shift to a harder domain.

  • Intermediate

    Engagement

    Moderate error is shrinking. Explore and consolidate.

  • Too complex

    Frustration

    Error stays high without reduction. Limit inquiry or ask for help.

The Wundt/Goldilocks curve is a heuristic, not a universal law. The zone shifts as schema competence changes.

08 · Basal-ganglia gating

A default brake.
Selective release.

  • Default brake

    Suppress

    Keep candidates and distractions from overwriting the active goal.

  • Selective release

    Open one gate

    Admit a chosen item into working memory or release a prepared action.

  • Veto / emergency hold

    Raise threshold

    Suppress competitors or hold everything when conflict is high.

This is a control primitive, not a literal three-pathway software circuit. PBWM is a model; the evidence is paradigm-specific.

PBWM, gating pathways, and evidence limits →08 / 12
09 · Input versus output gating

Think before speaking.

  • Input gate

    Admit

    Decide what enters working memory from perception or long-term memory.

  • Output gate

    Release

    Decide which held candidate drives speech, tool use, or action.

The system can hold contradictory candidates, evaluate them silently, and release only the winning synthesis. A context window has no built-in gate.

10 · Neuromodulators

Candidate variables,
not chemical equivalents.

  • Dopamine — outcome-versus-expectation, value, and gating. The clearest candidate.
  • Norepinephrine — unexpected uncertainty, reorientation, and interrupt/reset. Less direct.
  • Serotonin — horizon, patience, or restraint. More speculative.
  • Acetylcholine — sensory versus memory-prior weighting. Least direct as an LLM mapping.

A latent feature, a calibrated readout, and a control variable are different claims. Modulators interact and have different timescales.

11 · Integration

One shared control loop,
not many modules.

  • Valuation

    Error and mismatch

    Estimate error change, uncertainty, social calibration, and resource strain.

  • Gating

    Hold or release

    Decide what enters the workspace and what is emitted or acted upon.

  • Modulation

    Adjust gain

    Change learning, attention, horizon, or selection without rewriting everything.

The hard part is arbitration: social hunger, curiosity, task goals, and resource limits can conflict. Use event-driven and bounded controls.
