01 / Engineering Foundations

Vector representation paradigms

For this engineering representation, each named channel has a value from 0.0 (no activation on the application’s scale) to 1.0 (its chosen maximum). A resting baseline can be nonzero. These values are designed intensity controls—not automatically calibrated probabilities, physiological measurements, or the original study’s rating scale.

Design principle: Intensity channels versus categorical probabilities
  • Individually bounded channels: ei∈[0,1]. Scores need not sum to one. This permits mixed states such as elevated Sadness and Joy together. Individually controlled scores can still be statistically correlated.
  • Softmax simplex: ∑i=1Nei=1. Appropriate for a categorical probability distribution, but it makes scores compete for a fixed sum. That is a different quantity from the independently bounded intensities used here.
02 / Theoretical Survey

Comparative affective taxonomies

Psychology and affective neuroscience provide distinct, non-convergent frameworks for operationalizing emotional experience. Selection depends on whether an application prioritizes biometric tracking, facial action coding, or descriptive expressiveness.

Table 01 · Five frameworks compared
Framework Primary Thinkers Dimensions / Primitives Best Operational Fit Primary Theoretical Limits
Basic Emotion Theory Paul Ekman (1992) 6 discrete categories: Anger, Disgust, Fear, Joy, Sadness, Surprise. Computer vision, facial action unit tracking, animation rigging. Prototype labels alone do not capture all contextual or cultural variation. FACS codes facial movements; it is not itself a six-emotion taxonomy.
Psychoevolutionary Model Robert Plutchik (1980) 8 primary polar emotions arranged as 4 opposite axes; blends into secondary dyads. Therapeutic vocabulary, UX design, sentiment analysis. A useful conceptual structure, but dyads and polar axes are not established universal laws of neural mixing.
Circumplex Model (PAD / VAD) James Russell (1980), Albert Mehrabian (1974) 2–3 continuous neuro-affective axes: Valence, Arousal, (Dominance). Biometrics, heart-rate variability, wearable tech, continuous scalar tracking. Fails to distinguish structurally distinct feelings with matching coordinates (e.g., Anger and Fear).
Constructed Emotion Theory Lisa Feldman Barrett (2017) Dynamic brain construction combining interoception, past concepts, and language. Modern cognitive neuroscience, anthropologic modeling. High conceptual fluidity makes rigid computational schemas difficult to pin down.
Semantic Space Theory Alan S. Cowen & Dacher Keltner (2017) 27 distinguishable categories in the 2017 video self-report study, with gradients between them; later studies explore other spaces. Generative AI, nuanced affective computing, narrative generation. Counts and meanings depend on modality, stimuli, participants, and measurement. SST does not prescribe this document’s fixed 27-channel schema.
03 / Structural Blends

04 / High-Dimensional Mapping

The 27-dimensional continuous semantic space

Cowen & Keltner (2017) studied self-reported responses to 2,185 short videos and identified at least 27 distinguishable categories of reported experience connected by continuous gradients. The study used categorical ratings, free responses, and affective scales; it did not establish a universal inventory of 27 biological primitives.

The channels below borrow that vocabulary for an inspectable engineering state. Their descriptions are practical paraphrases, not diagnostic definitions or validated biological markers. The zero-based ordering and [0, 1] controls are implementation choices. Later studies extend the research beyond video.

Admiration00

Warm approval, respect, and positive regard elicited by another's virtuous skill or integrity.

Adoration01

Profound, reverent affection, emotional warmth, and devotion directed toward a loved one or ideal.

Aesthetic Appreciation02

Pleasurable engagement and perceptual captivation elicited by elegance, beauty, and artistic form.

Amusement03

Lighthearted humor, playfulness, and spontaneous mirth provoked by non-threatening incongruity.

Anger04

Antagonistic, reactive arousal triggered by deliberate boundary violations or goal frustration.

Anxiety05

Apprehensive, future-oriented tension regarding uncertain negative events or loss of control.

Awe06

Profound reverence and cognitive accommodation elicited by perceived conceptual or physical vastness.

Awkwardness07

Uncomfortable self-consciousness provoked by social clumsiness, dissonance, or minor friction.

Boredom08

Restless, flat disengagement resulting from under-stimulating environments or low perceived meaning.

Calmness09

Tranquil, centered equilibrium characterized by parasympathetic dominance and inner stillness.

Confusion10

Epistemic disorientation when incoming sensory or narrative evidence contradicts active models.

Craving11

Targeted, dopaminergic appetitive urge driving focused seeking behavior toward a specific resource.

Disgust12

Visceral rejection and revulsion directed against physical contaminants, spoilage, or moral degradation.

Empathetic Pain13

Vicarious distress and autonomic resonance provoked by witnessing the acute suffering of others.

Entrancement14

Hypnotic, absorbing immersion where conscious reflection softens into undivided attention.

Excitement15

High-energy sympathetic activation and buoyant anticipation toward positive incoming opportunities.

Fear16

Acute defensive alarm triggered by an immediate, perceptible threat to survival or psychological safety.

Horror17

Shocked revulsion combining intense dread, physical recoil, and moral paralysis.

Interest18

Curious, exploratory vigilance drawing cognitive resources to inspect novel or complex patterns.

Joy19

Radiant, flourishing happiness, celebratory relief, and profound emotional elevation.

Nostalgia20

Bittersweet, tender reminiscence and longing for past places, eras, or departed connections.

Relief21

Sudden release of autonomic distress following the verified conclusion or avoidance of a threat.

Romance22

Impassioned tenderness, infatuation, and reciprocal longing for deep, idealized interpersonal communion.

Sadness23

Dejected sorrow, reduced motor tempo, and sorrowful grief following irreplaceable loss.

Satisfaction24

Quiet fulfillment, satiety, and positive contentment following the successful completion of an intention.

Sexual Desire25

Visceral erotic drive, biological arousal, and physical attraction toward intimate partnership.

Surprise26

Brief, involuntary orienting reflex triggered by sudden deviations from expected stimuli.

06 / State Transitions

Dynamics and decay mathematics

In living systems, emotions do not vanish instantly; they exhibit emotional inertia. As an engineering approximation, each channel can relax toward a personal baseline, with an additional stimulus applied at the end of the time step.

Implementation note. The additive stimulus can push the update outside the intended [0, 1] range. Clamp the result, or use a bounded stimulus model, if every channel must remain in that interval.

07 / Beyond the original experiment

From a video study to Semantic Space Theory

The 2017 taxonomy was not simply discarded and replaced by one superior theory. It became part of a broader research program, formalized in Semantic Space Theory (Cowen & Keltner, 2021). SST asks how many distinctions people make, how those distinctions are conceptualized, and whether emotional meanings form clusters, gradients, or both.

Follow-up studies broadened the modalities and populations studied. They do not all measure the same thing: felt experience, recognized expression, and expression–context associations must be kept distinct.

What the changing counts mean

24, 12, 13, 16, and 27 describe different tasks, datasets, and constructs. They are not competing version numbers of one canonical taxonomy, and should not be added together or treated as interchangeable sets of biological primitives.

Neural evidence: informative, but not a 27-channel brain specification

Horikawa, Cowen, Keltner, & Kamitani (2020), published in iScience, measured fMRI responses in five participants watching 2,181 videos. The analyses used video annotations for 34 emotion categories and 14 affective dimensions, rather than directly reading out this document’s 27 controls.

Category-based models predicted responses better than the tested broad-affect models, and the authors reported distributed, category-related clusters bridged by gradients, especially in transmodal brain regions. This supports a richer account than a few broad affective axes, but does not prove 27 innate modules or causal mechanisms.

The study also leaves open whether some patterns reflect felt emotion or recognition that a scene affords an emotional experience. Its small participant sample and annotation-based targets matter when interpreting “neural validation.”

08 / Language, culture, and interpretation

A map of meanings is not automatically a map of universal biology

A constructionist interpretation, associated with Lisa Feldman Barrett and colleagues, emphasizes that emotion concepts depend on language, learning, and context. A lexical critique of semantic mapping asks whether recognizable structure reflects how participants conceptualize and describe experiences, rather than an invariant set of natural kinds.

That is a methodological question, not a reason to dismiss every self-report result as an inevitable artifact. The 2017 work included multiple response formats, and later modality studies test related questions with different stimuli. Conversely, high-dimensional prediction or cross-cultural agreement does not, by itself, settle whether categories are innate or constructed.

What 2,474 languages contribute

Jackson et al. (2019, Science) examined colexification—one word expressing multiple concepts—across 2,474 languages, 20 language families, and 24 emotion concepts. The lexical networks varied across families while also showing shared organization related to valence and physiological activation.

For example, the authors describe “surprise” as closely associated with “fear” in Austronesian networks, versus “hope” and “want” in Tai-Kadai networks. This is evidence about lexical meanings, not a direct replication or refutation of the 27-category video experiment. Shared expressive patterns can coexist with culturally different concept boundaries.

Expression, context, and the limits of inference

Barrett et al. (2019) reviewed evidence against treating a facial configuration as a context-free diagnostic marker of a specific emotion. The same movement can occur in different emotional or non-emotional situations, and similar emotions can be expressed differently.

Keep three claims separate
  • Descriptive structure: participants or models distinguish patterns in a particular dataset.
  • Generality: some of those patterns transfer across tasks, modalities, or sampled populations.
  • Mechanism: a fixed, universal biological cause explains those patterns. The first two claims do not automatically establish the third.

These sources illustrate an ongoing debate about interpretation; they are not presented as a single published constructionist rebuttal that conclusively overturned SST.

09 / Representation and operational control

Named channels, latent embeddings, or both?

The practical choice is not “old taxonomy versus a scientifically complete embedding.” It is a trade-off between inspectable semantics, learned representations, application requirements, and evidence for the intended task.

Commercial expression measurement: Hume AI

Hume AI, co-founded by Alan Cowen, is an industry example of high-dimensional expression measurement. Its API documentation describes separate speech and facial-expression outputs. Exact label inventories, model versions, and score definitions are vendor-specific—not a replacement standard for the 27-channel state used here.

The cited score documentation describes independent probabilities that do not sum to one. Those expression-related estimates are not automatically the same quantity as internal intensity controls, and the documentation cautions against comparing scores across names as though they were interchangeable. A face or voice model’s output is not a direct measurement of a person’s private feeling.

For an integration, record the model/API revision, output schema, score semantics, input provenance, and relevant calibration. Do not hard-code a label inventory without pinning a release, or feed expression probabilities directly into the intensity-update equation without defining a mapping.

Latent representations: useful features, not word-free emotional truth

CLIP learns image–text representations from paired supervision; ImageBind learns a joint space across six modalities using image-paired data. These are examples of learned multimodal representation, not simply unsupervised emotion taxonomies or emotion-specific models by default.

Vector widths such as 512, 768, or 1024 are architectural choices, not counts of emotions. Individual coordinates usually lack a stable human-readable interpretation. Similarity can support retrieval and downstream affective models, but may also reflect objects, scenes, language, or recording conditions rather than affect.

Recommendation for this repository

Keep the 27-channel schema when inspectability and deliberate agent control are the goal. Add a separate, versioned latent representation only when perception or retrieval needs it. Treat the two as complementary layers, not interchangeable measurements.

Keep source observations, model confidence, and the designed internal state distinct. If a model estimates a person’s expression, preserve uncertainty, context, and consent; do not turn an expressive UI control into a claim of validated emotional assessment. There is no evidence here that 27 channels are optimal for every application.
